# Sector Knowledge Base: Solar & Home Improvement

## Sector Overview

Solar and home improvement encompasses businesses that sell and install high-value products for residential properties. Unlike SMB local services (which are typically repair/maintenance), this sector focuses on considered purchases with longer sales cycles, higher deal values, and more complex regulatory environments.

The marketing approach differs significantly from local services: the focus is on lead nurturing over weeks/months rather than immediate response to emergency needs.

## Industry Pain Points

### Lead Generation Pain Points

**"Leads cost too much"**
- Translation: Cost per acquisition is eating margins, especially for solar where CPAs can reach $3,000-5,000.
- Root cause: Reliance on expensive lead aggregators, poorly optimized paid media, or over-investment in door-to-door without digital support.
- Typical numbers: Solar CPL from aggregators is $100-250/lead with 5-15% close rates, making CPA $700-5,000.
- Solution: Diversify lead sources -- combine owned media (SEO, Google Ads) with earned (referrals) and reduce aggregator dependency. Targeted Facebook/Instagram ads can deliver $30-80 CPL for solar with proper creative and landing pages.

**"Homeowners ghost us after the quote"**
- Translation: Prospects get multiple quotes and go silent. Low close rates.
- Root cause: No structured follow-up process, slow quote delivery, no differentiation from competitors.
- Typical numbers: Industry average close rate is 15-25% for solar, 20-35% for windows/siding.
- Solution: Speed-to-lead (quote within 24 hours), automated nurture sequences (email + SMS), comparison tools that position your offer against competitors, financing pre-qualification.

**"We can't scale beyond canvassing"**
- Translation: Door-to-door sales is their primary channel and it doesn't scale efficiently.
- Root cause: Canvassing is labor-intensive, weather-dependent, and limited by geographic reach.
- Solution: Digital lead gen to supplement canvassing. Use canvassing data (neighborhoods, demographics) to inform digital targeting. Build a digital pipeline that generates leads while canvassers are off.

### Operational Pain Points

**"Permits are a nightmare"**
- Translation: Permitting delays extend project timelines and frustrate customers.
- Root cause: Varies by municipality. Some jurisdictions take 2-4 weeks for solar permits.
- Marketing impact: Set realistic timelines in marketing materials. "Installed in X weeks" claims must account for permitting.
- Solution: Not a marketing fix, but marketing can set expectations. Highlight permit handling as a service differentiator.

**"We lose deals to cheaper competitors"**
- Translation: National competitors (Tesla/SunRun for solar) or big box stores (Home Depot for windows) undercut on price.
- Root cause: Cannot compete on price alone with companies that have national scale.
- Solution: Differentiate on quality, warranty, local service, speed, and personalization. Marketing should emphasize value over price.

**"Our sales team isn't closing enough"**
- Translation: Sales training and process issues, not just a marketing problem.
- Root cause: Inconsistent pitch, no CRM discipline, poor lead qualification.
- Marketing impact: Better lead qualification means sales team gets warmer leads. Implement lead scoring.
- Solution: Marketing can pre-qualify leads with qualifying questions on forms, automated education sequences before the sales appointment.

## Sales Cycle Analysis

### Typical Solar Sales Cycle (45-90 Days)

```
Day 0:   Lead captured (form fill, call, canvass appointment)
Day 0-1: Speed-to-lead response (call within 5 minutes)
Day 1-3: In-home consultation / site assessment
Day 3-5: Proposal delivered (system design + pricing + financing)
Day 5-14: Prospect compares quotes (typically gets 2-3 quotes)
             -> CRITICAL: Automated nurture sequence active here
Day 14-30: Decision made (or lost)
Day 30-45: Contract signed, permit submitted
Day 45-60: Permitting (varies by jurisdiction)
Day 60-75: Installation scheduled and completed
Day 75-90: Inspection, interconnection, system activated
```

**Marketing's role in each stage:**

| Stage | Marketing Action | Content Needed |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| Lead capture | Compelling landing page, clear value proposition | Ad creative, landing page copy |
| Speed-to-lead | Automated SMS confirmation + appointment booking | SMS templates, booking page |
| Consultation | Leave-behind materials, digital proposal | Brochures, proposal templates |
| Quote comparison | Email nurture sequence, testimonials, FAQ | 5-7 email sequence, video testimonials |
| Decision | Urgency (expiring incentives), objection handling | Financing calculator, incentive deadlines |
| Post-close | Referral ask, review request | Referral landing page, review links |

### Key Metric: Speed-to-Lead

Research across the home improvement industry:

- Responding within **5 minutes** of lead submission: **21x more likely** to convert vs 30+ minutes.
- Responding within **1 hour**: 7x more likely to convert vs next day.
- Average solar company response time: **3.5 hours** (opportunity for competitive advantage).

**Actionable:** Implement automated SMS + call routing for all inbound leads. The goal is human contact within 5 minutes, or at minimum an automated text within 60 seconds.

## Regulatory and Compliance

### Federal: Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

- **Current rate:** 30% federal tax credit for residential solar installations (through 2032).
- **Step-down schedule:** 30% through 2032 -> 26% in 2033 -> 22% in 2034 -> 0% after 2034 (unless extended).
- **Marketing use:** The ITC is the single most powerful selling point for solar. "Save 30% on your solar installation with the federal tax credit."
- **Compliance:** Must clearly state it is a tax credit (not a rebate or discount). Cannot guarantee tax savings (depends on individual tax situation). Recommend "consult your tax advisor."

### State and Local Incentives

| Incentive Type | Examples | Marketing Use |
|---------------|---------|--------------|
| State tax credits | Varies by state | Stacks with federal ITC |
| Net metering | Most states (varies) | "Sell excess power back to the grid" |
| SREC (Solar Renewable Energy Credits) | NJ, MA, DC, others | Additional revenue stream |
| Property tax exemption | Many states | "Solar won't increase your property taxes" |
| Sales tax exemption | Many states | "No sales tax on solar installation" |
| Utility rebates | Varies by utility | Reduces upfront cost |

### HOA Requirements

- Solar access laws in many states prevent HOAs from outright banning solar.
- However, HOAs can still regulate placement, angle, and aesthetics.
- Marketing should acknowledge HOA considerations: "We handle HOA approvals for you."

### Permitting

- Building permits required in virtually all jurisdictions.
- Electrical permits for solar and major electrical work.
- Some jurisdictions require separate structural, electrical, and solar permits.
- Timeline: 1-6 weeks depending on jurisdiction.

### Advertising Rules

- Cannot guarantee energy savings without site-specific analysis.
- "Zero electric bills" claims are risky; "dramatically reduce" is safer.
- Financing terms must include APR and standard disclosures (Reg Z for consumer credit).
- Before/after energy bill comparisons must be representative and documented.

## Financing Considerations

### Solar Financing Options

| Option | Description | Customer Monthly | Ownership | Tax Credit | Best For |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Cash Purchase** | Pay upfront | $0 after purchase | Customer owns | Customer claims ITC | Highest ROI, fewest customers |
| **Solar Loan** | Secured loan, 10-25 years | $80-200/mo | Customer owns | Customer claims ITC | Most common, best value |
| **PPA (Power Purchase Agreement)** | Buy the power, not the system | Pay per kWh (lower than utility) | Third party owns | Third party claims ITC | No upfront cost, lower savings |
| **Lease** | Monthly lease payment | $80-150/mo | Third party owns | Third party claims ITC | Predictable costs, lower savings |
| **PACE Financing** | Property-assessed, attached to home | Added to property tax | Customer owns | Customer claims ITC | No credit check, but controversial |

**Marketing guidance:**
- Lead with monthly savings comparison: "Your current electric bill: $200/mo. Your solar payment: $140/mo."
- Always mention the 30% ITC for cash and loan options.
- For PPA/lease, emphasize "no upfront cost" and "lower rate than your utility."
- PACE financing: be careful. Some states have restricted or banned PACE for residential. Verify local regulations.

### Home Improvement Financing

| Option | Typical Terms | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Same-as-cash (0% for 12-18 months) | No interest if paid in full within promo period | Customers who can pay quickly |
| Low-interest installment | 5-15% APR, 3-10 year terms | Larger projects ($15K+) |
| Home equity loan/HELOC | Variable rate, longest terms | Major renovations |
| Personal loan | 6-20% APR, shorter terms | Smaller projects, no home equity |

## Competitor Landscape

### Solar: National vs Local

| Competitor Type | Examples | Strengths | Weaknesses | How to Compete |
|----------------|---------|-----------|------------|---------------|
| **National companies** | SunRun, Sunpower, Tesla | Brand recognition, financing, scale | Generic service, long wait times, pressure sales | Emphasize local service, faster installation, personalized design |
| **Regional installers** | Varies by market | Local expertise, relationships | Limited marketing budget | Differentiate on quality, warranty, reviews |
| **Franchise/dealer networks** | Titan Solar, Momentum | Brand + local presence | Inconsistent quality across dealers | Focus on your specific track record |

### Home Improvement: Big Box vs Local

| Competitor | Threat Level | Their Advantage | Your Counter |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Home Depot / Lowe's | High for windows, moderate for other | Brand trust, financing, volume pricing | Quality of product, installation expertise, warranty |
| Bath Fitter / Re-Bath | High for bathroom | Franchise marketing power, speed | Custom work, better materials, local reputation |
| Pella / Andersen (direct) | Medium for windows | Manufacturer direct | Installation quality, service after sale |
| Local competitors | Varies | Price competition | Reviews, case studies, specialization |

**Key differentiator for local companies:** Reviews and reputation. A local installer with 200+ Google reviews and a 4.8 rating wins over a national brand with generic reviews, especially for high-ticket decisions where trust matters most.
